    August 2025 · about 27 by 17 km

    Up close

    A moonscape of pits and pools, each left behind once the gold was taken.

    The mercury used to bind the gold washes into the rivers, the fish and the drinking water.

    Along the highway, camps and towns have grown. In mining camps like these, Not For Sale has documented forced labor, child labor and sexual trafficking.

    Where the forest still stands, every road and every river is a way in.

    A satellite can measure the forest that is gone. It cannot see who was made to cut it.

    Many of the people doing this work did not choose it. Indigenous families trapped in debt are forced into the mines, destroying the forest their own communities depend on.

    The same system that traffics people destroys the forest.

    About this story

    One system, not two crises

    Ecocide destroys the land people live from; displacement and desperation follow, and traffickers exploit them. In Madre de Dios the chain also runs the other way: people trapped in debt are made to carry out the destruction. Not For Sale works on both at once.

    “Unlawful or wanton acts committed with knowledge that there is a substantial likelihood of severe and either widespread or long-term damage to the environment being caused by those acts.”

    The legal definition of ecocide, written in 2021 by an independent panel convened by the Stop Ecocide Foundation
