    DownstreamUpstream

    Downstream

    Here is rescue: shelters, hotlines, emergency care. It saves lives. But by the time someone is here, the harm has happened.

    Not For Sale’s view is blunt: if you only show up after people are trafficked, you are already too late.

    Root causes

    Walk upstream and you see why people end up in the river: no safe work, debt, unsafe migration, no papers.

    And damaged land. When forests, rivers and harvests fail, livelihoods fail with them, and traffickers move in.

    Upstream intervention

    Acting before harm occurs: helping children stay in school, creating safe work, restoring ecosystems, building income where illegal economies dominate.

    Here, the boats stay moored. Not because someone pulled them out, but because nothing pushed them in.

    The words behind the work

    For Not For Sale, social innovation means out-creating the conditions that drive modern slavery and ecocide.

    In anti-trafficking work, that can mean safe jobs for survivors, ethical supply chains or dignified livelihoods. Dignita in Amsterdam is one.

    Poverty, displacement, lack of safe work, conflict, discrimination, unsafe migration, climate pressure, environmental destruction.

    Education, safe housing, ethical jobs, legal identity, responsible supply chains, digital safety.

    In trafficking prevention: unsafe work, debt, missing documents, weak labor protections, environmental collapse, and demand for cheap labor.

    A survivor-centered enterprise does not use survivors as symbols. It creates real paths to income, leadership and independence.

    It rejects the idea that experts alone should decide what communities need.

    Not how many people attended a workshop, but whether people became safer, freer and less vulnerable to exploitation.

    About this story

    Out-create the conditions

    Human trafficking and ecocide grow where people are poor, displaced, in debt, without papers or safe work, and where the land they live on is being destroyed. A purely reactive model arrives after the harm. Social innovation asks a different question: what would need to exist so that exploitation becomes less likely in the first place?
